01 the problem

Ticketing is rented, not owned.

  • Attendees hold a barcode in someone else's app. Screenshot it and it's forwarded; lose the account and it's gone.
  • Organizers earn nothing on resale. Scalpers capture the upside on exactly the events that sell out.
  • Transfers happen off-platform, so nobody can prove who holds what at the door.

02 what it does

A ticket is an NFT in the attendee's own wallet.

  • Organizers mint and sell. They set a royalty and earn it automatically on every resale.
  • HubWorld never holds a user's key. Every movement of a ticket or of money is signed by its owner, on their own device.
  • Admission is a signature, not a screenshot. A QR can be forwarded; a signature needs the attendee's key at that moment.

05 findings

What we measured that nobody had written down.

Royalty is charged after the broker fee

5% → 4.875%

The issuer receives 5% of the bid minus the platform fee, not of the headline price.

Reserve is per page, not per token

0.2 XRP / 32

A 32-ticket event locks 0.2 XRP in reserve, not 6.4.

MPT removes the bottleneck, destroys the royalty

paid in tokens

One signature issues thousands — but its transfer fee is charged in tokens, not XRP.

06 the field

NFT ticketing is not an unproven idea.

GET Protocol processes over two million tickets a year. GUTS, YellowHeart and others run at festival scale. What is unproven is doing it natively on the XRP Ledger.

  • The royalty is enforced by the ledger, not by a marketplace's goodwill. Elsewhere it is contract-enforced and can be routed around.
  • No smart contracts. The whole settlement model is three native transaction types — nothing to audit, upgrade or exploit.
  • No custody at any point, including the attendee's ticket, which keeps working whether or not HubWorld does.

07 limitations

What doesn't work yet.

  • No users. The mainnet event used four accounts we control.
  • Production runs testnet. The live site is a demonstration.
  • Onboarding isn't self-serve. The royalty must pay the issuer, so every organizer is their own issuer — and arrives as an account wallets have never seen.
  • Events cap in the low hundreds, because the organizer signs each mint.

Stated up front because a reviewer who finds an unstated limitation afterwards discounts everything else.
